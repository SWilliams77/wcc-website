Built for the Room You Actually Have

Most homes aren’t perfectly square, level or standard-sized, and good work doesn’t pretend they are. A cabinet might need to meet a wall that isn’t plumb, match an older face frame, or tuck in beside an appliance with little room to spare. A built-in might have to make sense of an awkward alcove.

That’s the work we’re built around: custom cabinets and islands, built-in shelving, closets, doors and trim, along with the bathrooms, decks, sunrooms and floors that often come with them. Whether it’s one replacement cabinet or a whole room, the goal is the same. New work should look like it belongs.

How it works

From First Conversation to Finished Fit

  1. 1

    Step 1: Tell us about the space

    Send a few photos and a short description of what isn’t working or what you’d like to build. Rough measurements help, but they aren’t required.

  2. 2

    Step 2: We see the space in person

    We measure and look at the walls, floors, trim and fixtures the new work will connect to, so your estimate is based on the real room, not a guess.

  3. 3

    Step 3: You get a written estimate

    It spells out the materials, quantities, labor and finish work, so you know what’s included.

  4. 4

    Step 4: We plan the work

    We settle the timing, the order of the work and any other trades involved before the first day on site.

Estimates are written out line by line: materials, dimensions, quantities, labor and finish work. That detail matters most when new cabinets or trim have to meet existing walls, floors, doors and fixtures, and it means fewer surprises once the work starts.

  • The materials and quantities behind the price

What Is Architectural Millwork?

It’s a formal name for something simple: woodwork that’s built into the room instead of bought for it. Built-in bookshelves, a fireplace wall, a pantry cabinet that runs to the ceiling, closets, doors and trim all count as millwork when they’re designed around the space.

The result is a room that makes good use of its space and looks finished, without awkward gaps or pieces that feel like an afterthought.
